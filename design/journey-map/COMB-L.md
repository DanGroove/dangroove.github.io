What causes it (traced in the cloud and on Dan's PC; the measurements of lag-3 are in `journey-map-runs\lag-3`):

1. **The finds' idle motion repaints the whole map.** At rest the ravens' bodies and heads, the wayfinder's needle, the serpent's pad, the beacon's beam and flame, Nidhogg's head, the hoards' glints, the well's ripples and the bits rewrite attributes inside `svg#map` every frame. `svg#map` is one composited layer (reason Overlap), so each frame re-rasters its tiles under them, and each tile replays the sea, the land, the coast and the detail beneath.
2. **The beacon's beam redraws the sea, the mist and the clouds.** At every stop `findsheets.mjs` calls `depthBeam` with a new angle each frame; `depthBeam` sets `DP.force`, so `depthFrame` redraws `#deep` (its rings, waves, rays and about 240 dust squares), `#air` and the clouds in `#sky` every frame. Only `#air`'s veil uses the beam. With the beam frozen, `#deep` stops redrawing at rest and `#air` drops to its idle rate.
3. **The cards never settle.** In `cardsFrame`, `c.tilt` eases toward its target but snaps only to 0. A closed card's target is `cfg.tilt` and an open card's comes from the pointer's look, so the tilt changes by a tiny amount every frame forever: every frame rewrites the faces' and shadows' transforms and the open card's tail path (`svg.c-tail`). The open card's shadow box (`div.c-shadow`, a composited layer of 761 x 465 at Laurier) is repainted about 60 times a second at rest, because the blurred `.c-sh` inside it is moved by the tilt every frame.
4. **The step bar's class** (`#go`) is written every frame.
5. **The first glide's stop is a layer split.** When a large, sparse group inside `svg#map` has a group opacity between 0 and 1, the browser splits `svg#map` into 10 to 25 extra composited layers, and joins them again when the opacity reaches 0 or 1; each split and join re-rasters all of them. In the first glide the tier-2 labels fade about 1 s after the press, for about 0.4 s (35 layers become 54 to 56, then 35 again): that is the 130-230 ms stop, with about 70 tile rasters, new tile textures and shader compiles in one frame. Probed at rest (1440 x 900): an opacity of .5 on `.map-tier2` adds 25 layers (.999 does too), `.map-tier1` 10, `#finds` 16, `#b-detail` 20, `#b-fill` 9, `#b-all` 18; on `#b-sea`, `#b-coast`, `g.ygg`, `#trail-done`, `#fxg`, `#f-ravens`, `#f-runestone`, or on each tier-1 label's own `text`, it adds none. Forcing `.map-tier2` and `.t2b` to opacity 1 removes the first glide's split; forcing their visibility alone does not.
6. **Glides repaint everything every frame.** In steps mode `tripApply` rewrites `#cam`'s transform every frame, so all of `svg#map` re-rasters every frame (30 to 43 ms of GPU work per frame in Chrome at Dan's size).
7. **Explore's zoom tail repaints every frame.** After a wheel notch or a pinch the camera eases to its target; once it moves less than `FRAME_PERF.sharpStep` per frame, `exploreSharp` makes `moving` false and the map re-rasters every frame until it stops.
8. **The stars' discs are filled as one path per bin.** From the blast on `#stars` redraws on every frame (its stars twinkle and orbit), and `flush` (`stars.mjs`) fills each colour and opacity bin's discs as one path spread over the whole screen. On Dan's Chrome such a path costs about the area of its box, so each redraw takes about 20 ms of GPU work at his size, and explore runs at 39 fps even at rest. `#deep`'s rings (six long paths around the coasts), its rays (one path across the screen) and its even-odd clip cost the same way: about 10 ms per moved frame together.

What lag-4 and lag-5 showed on Dan's Chrome: a path costs roughly in proportion to the area of its box on screen, unless it is a rectangle, or a circle, an ellipse or another convex shape filled as a path of its own, which the GPU draws directly (that last part is the basis for the rays below; the timing pass checks it). When the frame covers the whole screen (`depthFrameCovers`), the rays and dust are skipped altogether (they draw nothing there today).
