## 1. Additional questions written before inspecting the product

1. Can a first-time visitor tell whether arrow keys act on the journey, a focused control, or a game?
2. Does keyboard ownership change safely when a sheet or game opens?
3. If a modal closes, does the next key act only on the returned context?
4. Can every discovery be reached without knowing its name or using debug hooks?
5. Do hidden controls disappear from the focus order and accessibility tree?
6. Does Escape dismiss only the topmost layer, without also changing the journey?
7. Is browser Back distinct from the map's Back, and is the intended one clear?
8. Can the journey be resumed after closing its current card without repeating the whole camera move?
9. Is a fast second press ignored, queued, or executed, and can the visitor predict which?
10. Does clicking a card's content accidentally activate what is underneath it?
11. Do button labels describe their result at that particular beat?
12. Is progress announced without repeating the whole page or interrupting reading?
13. Do finds remain understandable after a partial interaction and reopening?
14. Is there a route out of an unfamiliar game before starting it?
15. Does losing, winning, restarting, or abandoning the game restore a consistent map state?
16. Do game instructions match the actual mouse, touch, and keyboard controls?
17. Can a touch visitor move and fire without the browser intercepting the gesture?
18. Is the distinction between a decorative object, a discovered object, and an actionable object perceivable without hover or colour?
19. Do tooltips and instructions remain long enough to read without forcing a camera hold?
20. Do unrelated timers continue behind a sheet or after its closure?
21. Does changing review settings preserve the current story state and focus?
22. Does the joined page release its scroll lock when the visitor returns to the hero?
23. Does focus ever move the browser viewport while the camera is telling a different story?
24. At extreme zoom, can the visitor recover a useful overview without resetting discoveries?
25. Are text scaling and the smallest supported viewport compatible with all essential actions?
26. Are there unlabelled SVG, canvas, or icon controls that hide meaning from assistive technology?
27. Does reduced motion remove involuntary camera travel and continuous effects, including late-created content?
28. Does resizing during an interaction preserve the selected stop, opened sheet, and usable hit targets?
29. After the tab returns from the background, do clocks resume fairly rather than skipping a game or story beat?
30. Do disabled controls actually reject all input methods, and is their disabled state conveyed?
31. Does the final call to action follow from the story and remain available after exploring?
32. Are there contradictory state indicators between the map, mini-map, timeline, finds count, and active card?
33. Are runtime errors, missing local assets, or unhandled promises silently degrading an apparently working screen?
34. Does any duplicate implementation leave one input path behaving differently from another?
35. Can a visitor select, drag, wheel, or swipe accidentally and violate the intended interaction contract?
36. Are repeated effects bounded so rapid interaction cannot accumulate invisible work or destroy responsiveness?
37. Does focus stay visible against every active card palette and bright animation state?
38. Are the map's spatial metaphors consistent when using nonspatial inputs such as Tab or screen-reader commands?
39. Is discoverability earned by the drawing and local response, or does the visitor need a global instruction to guess?
40. Does the page recover from cancelled pointer gestures without leaving capture, dragging, or stuck states?

## 6. Top findings (under 300 words)

The most immediate touch problem is target size: Dan's overview has 14 px shields and roughly 15 px hammer/chest targets; even the joined Start button is only 14.39 px high. The detailed artwork can stay, but its real hit areas need to grow.

Short viewports expose an overlooked composition failure: at 1024 x 600 the subtitle escapes the frame and the legend collides; landscape is much worse. Yggdrasil also still needs joined, tapered branches and less puff-like foliage, preserving its scale and detail.

The most serious keyboard issue is hidden state. At end, invisible stop buttons remain exposed, and Tab can land on an invisible Lennard shield that still opens its card. Popup dismissal drops focus to body; ordinary card entry can leave it on the vanished shield, and leaving from card content also loses focus. Light card headers almost erase the close-button focus outline.

The timeline has two separate state failures: a stale visible tooltip during ordinary key navigation, and broken arrow/current-value behavior in reduced motion. Changing the motion preference while the page is open is ignored.

Motion polish is incomplete despite the passing tests: interrupted rune closure jumps to full size, popups cut away, and manual reel dismissal bypasses its iris exit. Early touch dismissal from sheets in Explore also needs a local Close.

All nine existing tests passed; both pages and all eleven finds ran. These findings come from additional rendered, keyboard, touch and state checks, with known issues and unverified device/game states kept separate.
